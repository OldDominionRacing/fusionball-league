# Fusionball Rulebook — Draft v0.1

*Working draft, September 2026. Three kinds of text:*
- ***FIXED*** — decided; changes here change the sport.
- ***PROPOSED*** — a specific rule to test in the fall playtests; adopt, adjust, or drop off what the field shows.
- ***OPEN*** — no rule yet; must be resolved before Rulebook v1.0 is frozen for public play.

---

## 1. The Field

**1.1 Layout — width FIXED, length OPEN.** Football-style field with two end zones, played at **full soccer width** (about 76 yd at Ragain). Length and end-zone depth to be set from the playtests.

**1.2 Goal structure — FIXED.** Each end has one integrated goal: regulation football uprights with a **target ring** mounted to the crossbar. The uprights, the ring, and the end zone together form one scoring system.

**1.3 Target ring — FIXED geometry, size under test.**
- Vertical, in the plane of the uprights, resting on the crossbar: bottom of the ring at crossbar height (10 ft).
- Working diameter **4 ft** inside. *The playtests bracket 30, 36, and 48 in. and choose by make rate (see §12).*
- Clamped to the crossbar in stadium play; freestanding on a ballasted stand at the same height elsewhere.

**1.4 Target line — PROPOSED.** A line across the field at *[20]* yards from the goal line. A target score counts only if the ball is released from behind the line. Inside the line, only the touchdown and the uprights are available.

**1.5 Markings — OPEN.** Whether hash marks or lateral zones exist, and how the target line and point-after spot are marked.

## 2. The Ball — FIXED

An American-style football. Size and pressure per *[spec to be named]*.

## 3. Teams — FIXED

- **11** players on the field per team.
- **15** players on the active roster, plus one non-playing coach.
- No fixed offensive and defensive units. Players remain on the field through changes of possession.

## 4. Game Clock — FIXED

- **80 minutes** total on a fixed running clock: two halves, with a **15-minute halftime**.
- No stoppage time. The clock runs through incompletions, out-of-bounds, and scores except where §4.1 applies.

**4.1 Clock stoppages — OPEN.** Whether the clock stops for injury, the point-after set piece, or officials' reviews, and how the final minute of each half is handled.

**4.2 Overtime — OPEN.**

## 5. Continuous Play — FIXED

- There are no downs. Possession continues until the ball is taken by the other team, goes out of play, a score changes possession, or another defined stoppage occurs.
- A change of possession reverses the direction of play **immediately**. There is no reset, huddle, or substitution period.
- The ball may be carried, thrown forward, thrown backward, lateraled, or kicked at any point in live play.
- **No heading of the ball.**

**5.1 Possession clock — NOT ADOPTED.** Stalling is not expected to be a problem: defenders may pursue the ball carrier, so keep-away under pressure fails on its own — a slipped ball or a bad pass turns it over. No clock is imposed. *Watched in the playtests (§12); revisit only if a leading team demonstrates it can hold the ball out for the final minutes.*

**5.2 Forward-pass legality — OPEN.** Whether an incomplete forward pass is a turnover, a dead ball with possession retained, or handled by the possession clock. Any limit on the number of consecutive forward passes.

**5.3 Out of bounds — OPEN.** Restart method (throw-in, spot possession, or kick) and which team restarts.

## 6. Scoring — FIXED

Three scores from one goal:

| Score | Value | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Touchdown** | **6** | A player carries the ball across the goal line in possession, or legally receives it in the end zone. |
| **Target** | **3** | The ball is thrown through the target ring. *(Subject to §1.4 if adopted.)* |
| **Uprights** | **1** | The ball is thrown or kicked through the uprights above the crossbar. |

- **Higher value only.** A ball through the target has also passed between the uprights; it scores 3, not 4.
- The uprights and target may be attempted at any time in live play from anywhere on the field, including on the run. No set piece is required.
- **No touchdown by recovery.** A loose ball recovered in the end zone does not score. A touchdown requires possession carried in or a legal reception.

**6.1 Restart after a 1 or 3 in live play — OPEN.** Whether possession passes to the conceding team, restarts at a spot, or is contested.

## 7. Point-After — FIXED as a live continuation; details OPEN

- After a touchdown, the scoring team **must** attempt a point-after. There is no whistle and no set spot: **the point-after begins the instant the touchdown is scored, from wherever the ball is, with the defense still live.**
- The scoring team may run, pass in any direction, lateral, or kick, exactly as in open play. If the ring is ahead of the scorer, they may continue and put it through from close range for the **target (3)** — a short throw, or a **dunk**: a player may jump and put the ball through by hand. Otherwise they may pass back to a teammate to reset for a throw at the target or an attempt at the **uprights (1)**, thrown or kicked.
- **No touchdown on a point-after.** The goal line is not a scoring line during the attempt.
- **End-zone rules pause.** The backward-pass prohibition and the ball-on-ground possession rule of §8 do not apply during a point-after. A ball touching the ground during the attempt simply ends it.
- The attempt ends when a score is made, the ball is turned over, the ball carrier is tackled to the ground, the ball goes out of play, a forward pass is incomplete, or the ball touches the ground. A turnover during a point-after does not score for the defense and is not returned.
- A touchdown therefore resolves to **6, 7, or 9**.

**7.1 Dunking — FIXED.** A player may jump and put the ball through the ring by hand. Players may not be lifted or supported by teammates to reach the ring, on offense or defense. **Hanging on the ring, or using it for support, revokes the score** and ends the attempt; the same applies in open play.

**7.2 Clock — OPEN.** Whether the game clock runs during the point-after (it is required, so a running clock costs the scoring team time) or pauses.

**7.3 Restart after the point-after — OPEN.** Kick, throw-off, or possession to the conceding team at a spot.

## 8. The End Zone — FIXED

The end zone resolves attacks; it is not a possession sanctuary.

- A player in possession in the end zone may not pass backward across the goal line to retain possession.
- If the ball touches the ground in an end zone, possession resolves to the team defending that end zone.
- Deliberately knocking or kicking a loose ball into the end zone gains nothing (see §6, no touchdown by recovery).

**8.1 Legal reception in the end zone — OPEN.** Feet, control, and ground-contact standard for a catch that scores.

## 9. Turnovers and Loose Balls — FIXED

- An interception is live immediately; the intercepting team attacks at once.
- A fumble is live and may be recovered by either team, except as limited by §8.

## 10. Contact — OPEN

Legal tackling, blocking, and jersey-grabbing; what is prohibited; protection for a player in the act of throwing or kicking. *Fabric grab-resistance for the game jersey depends on this rule.*

**10.1 Preseason contact — PROPOSED.** Self-officiated preseason games are played at touch or wrap-tackle level; full contact is reserved for refereed play.

## 11. Offside, Substitutions, Penalties, Officials — OPEN

- **Offside** — whether any restriction exists on players ahead of the ball, and if so, how it applies to forward passing.

**11.1 Distribution rule — PROPOSED.** The field is divided into three equal thirds. At all times, each team must have at least *[3]* of its players in each third, the ball carrier included. If a team falls below the minimum in any third, possession turns over at the spot of the ball. *Purpose: prevent pack play; guarantee outlets deep and wide; keep throwing lanes to the ring open. Modeled on lacrosse's minimum-players-per-half rule, which officials enforce by counting.*

**11.2 Starter playbook — PROPOSED.** The league publishes three base formations satisfying §11.1 for year-one teams, so spacing is taught by the rulebook until coaching develops it.
- **Substitutions** — rolling or at stoppages; injury replacement within a 15-player roster.
- **Penalties** — the sanction menu (spot, distance, possession, sin-bin) and which infractions map to which.
- **Officials** — number, positions, and signals.
- **Goal-tending** — whether defenders may block the ring or uprights from directly in front, and from what distance.

## 12. Playtest Calibration Plan

The fall closed playtests exist to turn PROPOSED and OPEN items into FIXED ones. Measure, don't argue.

1. **Ring size.** Vertical rings at 30, 36, and 48 in., bottom at 10 ft. Ten throwers each throw ten balls from 15, 20, and 25 yards, no pressure. Record make rates. Then repeat with a defender closing.
2. **Live-play target rate.** Target: a clean look from typical range succeeds **about half the time**; under pressure, about a third. Choose the ring size (and target line, if adopted) that produces this.
3. **Point-after continuation.** Run point-afters as live continuations from real touchdowns against a full defense and record how often each option scores — dunk, throw at the target, reset and take the uprights. The rule set should leave the 3 and the 1 a real choice, neither automatic.
4. **Stalling — observe only.** No possession clock. Note whether a leading team ever manages to hold the ball out for the final minutes of a half under full pursuit. If it can't, §5.1 is closed for good.
5. **Contact.** Test jersey-grab rules against prototype kit at the tighter twist. Note tears and stretch after each session.
6. **Distribution rule.** Play with and without §11.1, and with minimums of 2, 3, and 4 per third. Count target attempts per possession and note whether packs form. Keep the minimum that produces the most attempts on the ring without making the game feel zoned.
7. **Roster fatigue.** Track substitutions and minutes per player across an 80-minute game with four subs; decide whether injury replacement needs a rule.
