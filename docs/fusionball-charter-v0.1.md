# Fusionball Charter — Draft v0.1

*Working document. September 2026. Sections marked **OPEN** are undecided.*

---

## Part I — Founding Principles

Fusionball exists to do three things. Every rule in this charter should trace back to at least one of them, and any rule that serves none of them should be questioned.

**1. Keep spectators entertained longer.**
Take the most compelling mechanics from other sports — continuous possession, immediate transition, a multi-value scoring decision the audience can read in real time — and remove the dead time that lets attention drift.

**2. Remove owner disinterest.**
An owner who doesn't have to care whether the team wins is a structural flaw in most professional sport. In Fusionball, the only people who can own a team are the people on it, and only while they are on it. Ownership ends when membership ends.

**3. Let every player earn at their level.**
A player should be able to make money at whatever level their ability places them, move up as they improve, move to a team that fits as they age, and never be reduced to a fourth-string bench role. Ability moves players; the structure moves money.

## Part II — The Game

A full rulebook (Rulebook v1.0) is a separate document. The charter fixes the elements that define the sport and cannot change without changing its identity.

**Fixed elements**
- Continuous-play contact field sport, American-style ball, football-style field. No downs.
- Eleven players per side on the field; fifteen on the active roster; one non-playing coach.
- Free ball movement: forward, backward, lateral, carried, kicked. No heading.
- Three simultaneous scoring values from one integrated goal structure:
  - **Touchdown — 6** (possession carried or legally received in the end zone)
  - **Target — 3** (ball thrown through the target)
  - **Uprights — 1** (ball thrown or kicked through the uprights)
- **Target hoop: 4 ft inside diameter, vertical, in the plane of the uprights, resting at crossbar height** — bottom of the ring at the crossbar (10 ft), center at 12 ft, top at 14 ft. Clamped to the crossbar in stadium play; freestanding on a ballasted stand for preseason and practice fields.
- A ball through the target scores 3, not 3 + 1: a throw through the ring necessarily passes between the uprights, and the higher value alone is awarded.
- **Every game is exactly 80 minutes** on a fixed clock: two halves with a 15-minute halftime. No stoppage time.
- Turnovers reverse play immediately. No reset, no unit change.
- The end zone resolves attacks; it is not a possession sanctuary. A loose ball reaching the ground in an end zone resolves possession to that end zone's team. No touchdown by recovering a loose ball.
- **After a touchdown, a required point-after** that continues live from the moment of the score, with the defense still in play: the scorer may carry on and dunk it — a short throw, or a jump to put it through by hand, no lifting, and hanging on the ring revokes the score (3) — or the team may pass back and reset for a throw at the target (3) or the uprights (1); no touchdown is possible during the attempt. A touchdown therefore resolves to 6, 7, or 9. *(OPEN: the restart after the attempt.)*

**Rulebook items still OPEN** *(must be frozen before any public competition)*
- Legal contact and blocking
- Offside
- Substitutions and injury replacement with a 15-player roster
- Exact field length and end-zone depth (playing width fixed at full soccer width)
- Point-after spot and the restart after it; restarts after 1- and 3-point scores in live play
- Kick mechanics during live play
- Penalties
- Goal-tending rules around the target
- Definition of an incomplete forward pass

## Part III — Teams and Ownership

### 3.0 Two forms of team — ledger and company
A team exists in one of two legal forms. The ownership rules in §3.1–3.6 are identical in both; only the paperwork differs.

**Ledger team (county tier; expected through year two).** The team forms no entity. The league keeps the team's unit ledger and holds its treasury in a league sub-account, acting as paymaster: distributions are paid directly to members per unit, with tax reporting by the league. The standard team agreement is a contract among the members and the league. Team-level sponsorships are signed through the league as agent for the team.

**Company team (required to hold a state-tier seat; expected from year three).** The team is an LLC formed by the league as part of promotion: filing, standard-form operating agreement, EIN, and access to a group CPA at a bulk rate. Units convert one-for-one from the ledger to LLC membership interests; treasury transfers in full.

A ledger team may elect to become a company team early. A company team may not revert.

### 3.1 Membership units
- Each team has **sixteen membership units**: fifteen players and one coach — held on the league ledger or as LLC membership interests per §3.0.
- **One person, one unit, one vote.** No individual may hold more than one unit on any team.
- Only a league-registered player, or the team's coach, may hold a unit. Ownership by anyone else — investors, sponsors, former players — is prohibited.
- A unit transfer is not valid until recorded in the **league registry**. The registry is how the ownership rule is enforced rather than trusted.

### 3.2 Founding buy-in
- Founding members pay a fixed buy-in into the team treasury, set at **cost recovery only** — sanction fees, registration, kit, and travel for the season. Nothing more.
- After founding there is no fixed price. Units sell at whatever a buyer and seller agree. A unit on a state champion is worth more than a unit on a county team. Players build value and take it with them when they sell.

### 3.3 Transfers
- A member may sell their unit to a recruit. The sale requires approval by **more than half** of the remaining members.
- The team holds a **right of first refusal**: it may buy the unit at the offered price and re-sell it to a recruit of its own choosing.
- A trade between teams is two sales — sell on Team A, buy on Team B, both teams vote, cash equalizes.
- **Transfer windows only.** No sales during a competition. Rosters freeze at the first game of each tier.
- **Financing** *(Principle 3)*: a team may sell a unit to a recruit on terms, paid from that player's future revenue share. The ladder is never pay-to-climb.

### 3.4 Exits
- A member who leaves and cannot find a buyer within the transfer window is redeemed by the team at **book value** (treasury ÷ 16). Same rule for injury, death (estate sells or is redeemed), or loss of league registration.
- **Removal** of a member against their will requires a **two-thirds vote** and payment at **fair value**, not book. Removing someone is deliberately harder than approving a sale.
- **Orders clause (military and equivalent government orders).**
  - *Temporary absence (deployment, TDY, training):* the member keeps the unit in **held** status for up to **fifteen months**. While absent the member has no vote, and the unit's distributions are paid to the **temporary player** the team registers into the roster spot. The temporary player holds no unit and no vote; they earn exactly what the held unit earns, for as long as they fill it. Vote and distributions return to the owner on return.
  - *Permanent relocation (PCS):* the member exits on the standard terms (sale within the window, otherwise redemption at book value), and the league records a **portable unit right**: first right to buy into a team in the new location at the same book value, or, where no team exists, a founder's kit to start one.

### 3.5 Distributions
- League revenue share and team gate flow to the treasury and distribute **per unit, equally**. Teams may vote bonuses out of their own funds.

### 3.6 Conduct and discipline
Two tracks, kept separate.

**Team track — "we don't want them."** A team's decision about its own members is the team's business. Removal requires the two-thirds vote and fair-value payment in §3.4. The league's only role is to record the resulting transfer.

**League track — conduct that affects the sport.** The league's sole instrument is **registration**. It never touches a unit directly; it suspends or revokes a player's registration, and the ownership consequences follow from the existing exit rules.
- **On-field discipline** (dangerous play, fighting, abuse of officials): game suspensions through a league disciplinary process. Registration, unit, and vote are untouched.
- **Off-field conduct** (illegal activity, conduct bringing the sport into disrepute): a written conduct policy with a defined standard, a hearing, and an appeal. Outcomes range from suspension to revocation.
- **Revocation**: the player is no longer eligible to hold a unit and has the standard window to sell. With no buyer, the team redeems the unit.

**Leaver pricing.**
- *Team removal* (§3.4): fair value — deliberately expensive, so a majority cannot strip a unit cheaply.
- *League revocation*: **book value** (treasury ÷ units). A player removed for conduct recovers their share of the treasury, not the appreciated value.

**Guard.** Because book value is cheaper than fair value, teams must have no role in revocation decisions, and revocation may only follow the defined standard through the defined process. The conduct policy, standard, and appeal body are published with the charter.

### 3.7 Legal notes
- The league publishes a **standard-form team agreement** (ledger form) and a **standard-form operating agreement** (company form). Every team adopts the one for its form. The ownership rule lives there and in the league charter.
- Selling units to recruits may raise securities questions in either form; the fact that every holder is an active participant is what keeps it clear. Both standard forms should say so explicitly and be reviewed by counsel before the first unit sale. The paymaster arrangement and its tax treatment for the league also need counsel review.
- **OPEN:** Is the coach's unit the same class as a player's, or a separate class with different transfer rules?

## Part IV — Competition Structure

### 4.1 The pyramid
Every team enters at the bottom and earns each seat above it by winning.

| Tier | What it is | How you get there |
|---|---|---|
| **County** | Entry tier. Local competition. | Form a team; register; pay buy-in. |
| **State** | Tournament of county winners. | Win your county. |
| **Lower National** ("State Champions Tournament") | Tournament of all state champions. | Win your state. |
| **Upper National** | Small league of incumbents. | Finish top two in Lower National. |

### 4.2 Promotion and relegation
- **Top two** of Lower National move up to Upper National.
- **Bottom two** of Upper National are relegated **to county** — not to Lower National. They must win their county and state again to return. National status is earned by playing, never held by tenure *(Principles 2 and 3)*.
- **OPEN:** Upper National size. Two up / two down only moves the pyramid if the league is small — six to eight teams recommended.

### 4.3 Definition of "county"
- Counties are wildly uneven. "County" is the language of the sport; the operational definition of the entry tier is **the smallest local unit with at least four teams**. **OPEN** for formal wording.

### 4.4 Activation by evidence, not calendar
The full structure is designed now but each tier activates when the team count supports it:
- **County competitions** open wherever four teams exist.
- **State tournaments** open when a state has at least four county-level teams. *(OPEN: threshold.)*
- **National tiers** open when at least eight states have champions. *(OPEN: threshold.)*

### 4.5 Calendar — OPEN
Three stacked tiers in one year is three seasons for players with jobs. Working assumption: county in spring, state in summer, national in fall, with Upper National playing a short season. To be decided.

### 4.6 Year One — Colorado Springs
- Team count open — as many teams as the Colorado Springs area can field. Four consecutive weekends: three weekends of pool play, championship on weekend four.
- Public, ticketed, with concessions or food trucks. Target venue: Ragain Field, 6303 Barnes Road (grass, soccer width, existing football uprights).
- This tournament **is** the first county competition. Its winner is the first county champion.

## Part V — League Economics

### 5.1 The league is cost-recovery
The league entity keeps **its operating costs** and passes everything else to teams. Operating costs are defined and published as exactly four things:
1. **Direct competition costs** — fields, officials, medical presence, insurance, event staff.
2. **Organizational overhead** — staff time, including the founder's, at a stated rate. Unpaid founder labor is a subsidy and should be visible.
3. **Reserve** — a fixed, capped percentage held for launching the next tier.
4. **License fee** to the IP holder for the mark, target design, ball specification, and platform — at a **stated rate**, not a residual.

Everything above these four goes to teams by finish position. Books are open to member teams.

### 5.2 What teams pay
- **Player registration** — annual license per player, priced to cover participant accident insurance plus platform costs. Scales with participation, not team wealth.
- **Team sanction fee** — per tier, per season, set at direct-cost recovery. Small, but it establishes that every seat is rented annually.
- **Registry fee** — flat fee to record a unit transfer. The league takes **no percentage** of unit sales; proceeds belong to the seller.

### 5.3 Where surplus comes from, by stage
| Stage | Sources |
|---|---|
| Year one | Ticketed admission, concessions or food-truck fees, and a presenting sponsor. Gate and concessions are the first revenue that actually reaches teams (Principle 3). |
| Multi-city | Local gate and sponsorship at every county competition. *(Required for Principle 3 — county money must exist, even if small.)* |
| Multi-state | Media rights (owned by the IP holder, licensed to the league), state and national gate and sponsorship. |

### 5.4 Team sales share — earning by bringing people
Word of mouth is how the sport spreads, and teams are paid for it — for **selling, never for recruiting.**

- Every team receives a **sales code**. A stated share of every ticket, season pass, merchandise item, or concession voucher sold through a team's code goes to that team's treasury. *(Working rate: **30% of the ticket price**; published in advance each season.)*
- The team share is deducted from gate before tier revenue is calculated. It belongs to the team that sold it, regardless of finish, and the league never touches it.
- Sales-share income distributes per unit like any other team revenue. Players earn as owners, at every level, by bringing people to games.

**Recruiting is rewarded, but never with money that a new team paid.** A team that brings a new team into the league may receive non-cash recognition or a credit against its own next-season sanction fee, funded from league sponsorship — one level only, capped, and never a share of any entry fee. Rewards tied to entry fees flowing upward are the definition of a pyramid scheme and are prohibited under this charter in every tier.

### 5.5 Where the founder makes money
Not the league. The league is the customer. Value accrues to the **IP company**, which sells to the league at published rates and to everyone else:
- **Equipment** — every sanctioned field needs a certified target; every game a certified ball. The real market is schools, rec leagues, and college clubs that want to play and must buy the target to do so.
- **License fee** from the league.
- **Media rights** across all tiers, as a condition of sanction. Given away until there's an audience worth selling.
- **Data platform** — player tracking, stats, broadcast overlays; registration and registry as a subscription; eventually data feeds to broadcasters and sportsbooks.
- **Marquee events** — the national championship promoted commercially by the IP company under league sanction.
- **International licensing** — other countries license the sport, governing body to governing body.
- **Merchandise** licensing.

Honest timing: years one through three are equipment margin and a sponsorship spread. That is true of every new sport regardless of structure.

## Part VI — Entity Structure

```
Lisandan Holdings, LLC        family holding layer (owns Epinath; operates nothing)
└── Epinath, LLC              venture holding layer; holds Fusionball IP
    ├── Old Dominion Racing   event operator; runs Year One under services contract
    ├── Fusionball League LLC competitions, insurance, sanctioning, registry, charter
    ├── ForgeKit
    ├── Tulendor / Wyrmrise
    └── Giragon
```

**Rules of the structure**
- **The league is a for-profit LLC** whose charter contractually commits it to the cost-recovery model in Part V. Cost recovery is enforced by contract with member teams, not by tax status. Colorado public benefit company status is available as a signal.
- **IP sits above the operating company.** Trademark, target design, ball spec, and rulebook text are held by Epinath and licensed down. If the league is sued out of existence, the sport survives.
- **Fusionball stays out of ODR.** ODR runs the year-one tournament as event operator and is paid for it. Fusionball equity never enters ODR's cap table.
- **Every venture in its own LLC, no shared bank accounts.**
- **Manager-managed** at every level, with the founder as manager, so that trust or holding-company ownership never removes operating control.
- **Future option:** if governing-body legitimacy ever matters (schools, colleges, international federations), spin the league into an independent or member-owned body that licenses the sport from Epinath. The IP company never moves.

**Immediate actions**
- Register the Fusionball trademark.
- File on the target design.
- Form Fusionball League LLC under Epinath.
- Draft the standard-form team operating agreement (counsel review).
